**Every large AI citation study so far has pointed the same direction: most citations go to domains the brand doesn't own. The first large study of *local* queries just found the opposite — on one engine.** Steady Demand's [AI Citation Ledger](https://www.steadydemand.com/ai-citation-ledger/), published in August 2026 and [covered by MarTech](https://martech.org/business-websites-dominate-geminis-local-ai-citations/), analysed 14,472 citations from 1,487 local search queries across 50 US metros and 10 service verticals. Gemini pointed 59.9% of its local citations at the business's own website. ChatGPT, on the same queries, gave businesses' own sites 15.9%.

**Key takeaways**

- Gemini's local citation mix: 59.9% business websites, 14.4% social and community forums, 10.4% local-service directories, 10.4% general directories. Businesses' own sites out-cited every third-party category combined.
- ChatGPT's mix is close to the inverse: 15.9% business websites, 41.7% forums, 34.6% general directories. The two engines cited the same domains in only 8% of query pairs and named the same top business 4.2% of the time.
- Reddit took 13.7% of Gemini's local citations — more than Angi, Thumbtack, and HomeAdvisor combined at 10.3%.
- The study's biggest finding is instability, not source mix. Repeating an identical Gemini query produced overlapping sources only ~40% of the time and the same top business only ~7% of the time, against ~90% consistency for Google's local pack. The authors call it Grounding Drift.
- Reviews are a bar, not a ladder: AI-recommended businesses averaged 4.75 stars against a 4.84-star market baseline, with no visible payoff beyond roughly 4.0.
- The long tail is wide open: 3,611 unique domains and 4,410 unique businesses were cited, most once or twice, and only 2% of businesses appeared in more than one metro.

## Local intent flips the citation mix — for Gemini

The headline inverts the finding we covered in [where AI citations come from](/blog/where-ai-citations-come-from). Muck Rack's 25-million-link analysis of national-topic prompts put 84% of citations on domains the brand doesn't own, and that figure has been stable for a year. Steady Demand's local-query data puts Gemini's business-owned share at nearly 60%.

| Source category | Gemini | ChatGPT |
|---|---|---|
| Business's own website | 59.9% | 15.9% |
| Social / community forums | 14.4% | 41.7% |
| General business directories | 10.4% | 34.6% |
| Local-service directories | 10.4% | 3.0% |
| Other | 4.9% | 4.8% |

Both things are true, and the reconciliation matters more than either headline. Citation source mix is a property of the query set and the engine. Ask about industry topics and the answers run on journalism, Wikipedia, and forums; ask "best plumber near me" and Gemini — grounded in Google's index and Business Profile data — reaches for the plumber's own site. Yext's location-based study, which found 44% of citations first-party, was an early sign of the same pattern; this study separates it cleanly by engine.

The engine split is the actionable part. A local business that reads the Gemini column and declares its website the whole game is invisible on ChatGPT, where 76% of citations went to forums and directories. And the two engines barely agree on anything downstream of the mix either: 8% domain overlap, 4.2% agreement on the top recommended business, ranging from 0.5% overlap in personal-injury law to 14.9% in auto repair. There is no single local AEO surface. There are per-engine surfaces, and per-vertical ones — dentists were cited through Zocdoc and Healthgrades, lawyers through Super Lawyers and Justia, and neither list transfers.

The Reddit result deserves its own line: 13.7% of Gemini's local citations, ahead of the entire local-service directory category. That is consistent with Reddit's continued weight in AI answers [despite its slide in ChatGPT's UI](/blog/chatgpt-ui-reddit-citations-api) — and a reminder that the [seeding-detection arms race](/blog/reddit-ai-citations) has now reached local services.

## Grounding Drift is the finding to sit with

The study's control tests are the part most coverage buried. Running the same Gemini query repeatedly produced overlapping cited domains only about 40% of the time — a result the authors backed with 5,000 pairwise comparisons and a 2,000-shuffle permutation test (p < 0.0005). Asking for the top recommended business, Gemini repeated itself about 7% of the time. Google's traditional local pack, tested the same way across 4,466 comparisons, repeated its top listing about 90% of the time.

Seven percent versus ninety. A local business that ranks first in the map pack holds that slot; a local business that gets recommended by Gemini this morning has mostly not been recommended by lunch. Fisher's framing in the report is that a single AI search should be read as a snapshot, not a result — which is the same conclusion our own six-week tracking reached as [citation volatility](/blog/citation-volatility), now replicated on local queries by an independent team with a different method.

The measurement consequence is blunt. Any local AI visibility claim built on one query run — yours, an agency's, or a tool's — is sampling a distribution and reporting a point. With 4,410 unique businesses cited and most appearing once or twice, presence in local AI answers is a *share* you estimate over repeated runs, not a rank you check. That is the case for [running prompt tracking on a schedule](/blog/ai-prompt-tracking) rather than auditing once a quarter.

## The review bar is a filter, not a ranking factor

One negative result worth the space: AI-recommended businesses averaged 4.75 stars and 919 reviews, but the baseline pool of local businesses pulled from the same categories averaged 4.84 stars. The businesses AI picks are not better-reviewed than the market — if anything, fractionally worse. Ninety percent of recommendations sat above 4.5 stars, but 14% had fewer than 50 reviews.

The read: ratings function as a threshold the market already clears, not a score to maximise. Past roughly 4.0 stars, the study found no relationship between review accumulation and citation frequency. Budget spent pushing 4.6 to 4.8 is budget not spent on the service pages Gemini actually cites or the vertical directories ChatGPT leans on.

## What this does not show

The usual discounts, plus a few specific ones. Steady Demand is a local SEO agency publishing research in its own category; the data argues for services it sells. The study is a snapshot of 50 large US metros and 10 service verticals — small-market and non-US behavior is unmeasured, and the authors themselves call the findings "not a fixed rule set." The Gemini numbers come primarily via API-grounded runs, and ChatGPT's repeat-consistency was not independently tested, so the Grounding Drift comparison is Gemini-versus-local-pack, not engine-versus-engine. Source categories reflect the authors' taxonomy, and — as the Muck Rack/Yext split shows — [taxonomy choices move these percentages](/blog/where-ai-citations-come-from). None of that threatens the two robust findings: the engines' source mixes are radically different, and repeat consistency is far below traditional search.

## What to do on Monday

1. **Split your local AEO plan by engine.** For Gemini: city-and-service pages on your own site, crawlable, current, specific. For ChatGPT: Reddit presence and the general directories it actually cites. The 8% overlap means one plan cannot serve both.
2. **Pull the cited domains for your vertical before buying listings.** The directories that matter are vertical-specific and the national names may not be on your list. Your category's actual citation ledger is the target list.
3. **Stop the review treadmill at the bar.** Hold 4.0+, respond to reviews, and reallocate whatever was budgeted for pushing 4.6 to 4.8.
4. **Re-baseline what "checking our AI visibility" means.** One run is a snapshot of a distribution with ~40% day-to-day source overlap. Fix a prompt set, run it on a schedule, and read multi-week shares.
5. **Watch the 2% club.** Only 87 businesses appeared in more than one metro. Local AI answers are not yet consolidated around chains and aggregators — which makes this the cheap phase to establish presence.

## Where this leaves local AEO

Local queries are the first category where a business's own website is demonstrably the dominant citation surface on a major engine — and simultaneously the category with the least stable answers ever measured. Both facts point at the same posture: do the on-site fundamentals, work the per-engine third-party surfaces, and measure with repetition instead of anecdotes. The businesses that win local AI search will be the ones that know their share of a moving distribution, not the ones that screenshot a good answer.
