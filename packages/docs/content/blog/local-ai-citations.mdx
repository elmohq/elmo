**Local search was supposed to be the safe corner of SEO — the local pack is stable, the ranking factors are known, and the playbook is twenty years old. The first large study of local AI citations describes a different machine entirely.** Steady Demand analyzed [14,472 citations from 1,487 local service queries](https://www.steadydemand.com/ai-citation-ledger/) across the 50 largest U.S. metros and found that nearly 60% of Gemini's citations point at businesses' own websites — more than every directory, review site, and forum combined — while the same query, repeated, [produces overlapping sources only about 40% of the time](https://martech.org/business-websites-dominate-geminis-local-ai-citations/). And in the middle of the study window, ChatGPT quietly rebuilt its local sourcing: Reddit went from 41.7% of its local citations to zero.

**Key takeaways**

- Business websites carry local AI answers: 59.9% of Gemini's local citations, versus 13.7% for Reddit and 10.3% for the entire local-directory category — Angi, Thumbtack, and HomeAdvisor combined.
- The engines disagree almost completely: Gemini and ChatGPT cited the same domains on 8.3% of identical queries and named the same top business 4.2% of the time.
- Consistency is the real finding. Identical Gemini queries overlap on cited domains ~40% of the time and repeat the same top business ~7% of the time; Google's local pack, run as a control, repeats its top listing ~90% of the time.
- ChatGPT's local source mix was discontinued mid-study: Reddit fell from 41.7% of citations to 0.0% after an 8 August retrieval change, while business websites jumped from 15.9% to 42.8%.
- Star ratings behave as a floor, not a ladder: 97% of AI-recommended businesses sit at 4.0 stars or better, but the average (4.75) is slightly *below* the plain-Google baseline (4.84).
- There is no universal local playbook — dentist queries leaned on Zocdoc and Healthgrades, lawyers on legal directories, and auto repair ran as high as 60% Reddit before the August change erased that channel on ChatGPT.

## The source mix nobody's local budget matches

Ask where local marketing money goes and the answer is listings: directories, aggregators, review platforms, profile management. Ask where Gemini's local citations go and the answer is the opposite end of the stack.

| Source type | Share of Gemini local citations |
|---|---|
| Business's own website | 59.9% |
| Reddit | 13.7% |
| Local-service directories (Angi, Thumbtack, HomeAdvisor, etc.) | 10.3% |
| Everything else (review platforms, media, forums, maps) | remainder |

The business website outdrawing *the combined directory category* by nearly six to one is the finding with a budget attached. Local visibility work has spent two decades optimizing intermediaries on the theory that the intermediary ranks and the business does not. A grounded engine composing an answer inverts that: it wants a page that says what the business does, where, and for how much, and the page most likely to say so is the business's own.

That reads as good news for small operators, and partly is — the study found [4,410 unique businesses cited across 3,611 domains](https://www.steadydemand.com/ai-citation-ledger/), a long tail no directory-dominated results page ever had room for. The catch is that it moves the burden onto the one asset most local businesses neglect. A five-page template site behind a well-tended Google Business Profile is precisely the wrong shape for this channel.

## Two engines, 4.2% agreement

The cross-engine comparison is where single-engine intuitions go to die. Running the same 1,487 queries through both engines, [Steady Demand found 8.3% average domain overlap and a 4.2% top-business match rate](https://www.steadydemand.com/ai-citation-ledger-gemini-vs-chatgpt/). The engines were not sampling the same pool differently; they were consulting different webs.

Before August, ChatGPT's local mix barely touched business websites (15.9%) and leaned on Reddit (41.7%) and general directories (34.6%). In some verticals the Reddit reliance was extreme — 72.2% of citations for dentist queries, 60.1% for auto repair, against 12.3% for personal injury lawyers, whose answers came from legal directories instead. Gemini, meanwhile, held business websites near 60% throughout.

Two practical consequences. First, "we checked ChatGPT and we look fine" is not a statement about local AI visibility; it is a statement about one engine, and the one whose sourcing just changed most. Second, category matters more than channel: the cited-source mix for a dentist and a lawyer in the same city have almost nothing in common, which is the same lesson the cross-industry data in [where AI citations come from](/blog/where-ai-citations-come-from) keeps teaching at the national level.

## The August rupture: Reddit to zero

Mid-study, the ground moved. Around 8 August 2026, ChatGPT's retrieval behavior changed, and [Promptwatch's tracking](https://promptwatch.com/blog/chatgpt-stop-citing-reddit) shows what happened underneath: fan-out queries using the `site:` operator — background searches scoped to a single named domain — jumped from 0.37% to 16.8% of all fan-out queries in a single day, and queries per response nearly doubled. ChatGPT started asking specific, named websites directly. Named official sites benefit from that pattern; discussion platforms are rarely the target of a `site:` query, and Reddit's share of ChatGPT Search citations fell from 3.83% to 0.52% — [an 86.4% drop in four days](https://searchengineland.com/reddit-chatgpt-search-citations-fall-report-485473), with no comparable move in Google's AI surfaces.

In the local sample, the same event was not a decline but an extinction:

| ChatGPT local citation source | 30 July | Post–8 August |
|---|---|---|
| Reddit | 41.7% | 0.0% |
| Business websites | 15.9% | 42.8% |
| General directories | 34.6% | 40.8% |
| Local-service directories | 3.0% | 11.6% |

A verification pass of 150 queries on 20 August found zero reddit.com citations in 334 total. Gemini's Reddit usage held steady through the same window, confirming the change was ChatGPT's, not Reddit's. Anyone who spent the spring seeding local recommendations into subreddits — the tactic whose risks were already visible in the [Reddit citation data](/blog/reddit-ai-citations) — watched that channel's ChatGPT value go to zero between two Mondays, which is [citation volatility](/blog/citation-volatility) expressed as a step function rather than a drift.

## Same question, different answer

The study's control experiment deserves more attention than its headline. Repeating identical queries, Google's traditional local pack returned the same top listing about 90% of the time. Gemini, on the same queries, overlapped on cited domains roughly 40% of the time and returned the same top business in about 7% of repeats.

Steady Demand calls this grounding drift, and their reading is the right one: **a single AI search is a snapshot, not a measurement.** The practical corollaries are uncomfortable for both directions of the trade. For businesses, ranking "number one in ChatGPT" for a local query is not a stable asset anyone can sell you, because the engine itself will not repeat the answer. For measurement, any tool — ours included — that queries an engine once per prompt and reports the result as your visibility is reporting one draw from a wide distribution. Sampling repeatedly and reporting rates is the only honest shape for the number, the same conclusion the volatility data forced at the national level.

The ratings finding fits the same picture of loose selection: recommended businesses averaged 4.75 stars against a 4.84 plain-search baseline, with 97% at 4.0 or better. Reputation is a gate the engine checks, not a scoreboard it sorts by. Past the gate, the draw is noisy.

## What this does not prove

**It is vendor research.** Steady Demand manages Google Business Profiles and local SEO for a living, and a study concluding that professional attention to local web presence matters is not against interest. The scale and the published splits — per-engine, per-vertical, before-and-after — make it checkable in a way most vendor decks are not, but the decimals are theirs.

**It is one country, one query family, one season.** Fifty U.S. metros and ten service categories, queried in mid-2026, with a major retrieval change in the middle of the window. The ChatGPT numbers in particular describe two different systems joined by a date; extrapolating either half forward is a guess.

**Citation share is not customer share.** Nothing here measures whether anyone called the plumber. A business website absorbing 60% of citations says where engines look, not what users do with the answer — and a business cited but not named, the [ghost citation](/blog/ghost-citations) pattern, earns nothing a customer can act on.

**The consistency numbers undermine the precision of everything else.** If the same query returns overlapping sources 40% of the time, then every percentage in the study is an average over a noisy process. That is an argument for reading the ordering and the magnitudes, not the second digit.

## What to do with it

1. **Reallocate toward the website.** Not a redesign — pages. A page per service per area that names the city, the service, the price range, and the proof. The engine citing "the business's own site" is citing a specific URL that answers the question asked.
2. **Map your vertical's cited sources before renewing directory spend.** Export the cited domains for your category's prompts from your own tracking. If Zocdoc carries dentist answers and Avvo carries lawyer answers, the generic directory subscription is funding a channel the engines in your category don't read.
3. **Sample, don't spot-check.** Run each tracked prompt repeatedly and report citation and recommendation *rates*. A [prompt set](/blog/ai-prompt-tracking) queried once is a lottery ticket; queried thirty times, it's a measurement.
4. **Track Gemini and ChatGPT as separate channels** with separate baselines. At 4.2% agreement, an aggregate "AI visibility" number for local is the average of two unrelated quantities.
5. **Keep review operations at threshold, not maximum.** Getting from 3.9 to 4.2 stars plausibly matters; getting from 4.6 to 4.8 is polishing past the gate the data shows.
6. **Assume the mix will break again.** The August change deleted a 40-point channel in a fortnight. Whatever share you measure this quarter is a reading with a date on it, and the alerting that catches the next step-change is worth more than the optimization tuned to the current one.

## The local pack was a promise; this is weather

The deeper shift is in what kind of system local visibility now is. The local pack was legible: deterministic enough to audit, stable enough to sell, contested enough to have a professional class attached. Local AI answers are none of those things yet — dominated by a source category nobody was optimizing, split between engines that agree 4% of the time, and rewritten by upstream retrieval changes with no announcement.

That is not an argument to ignore it. It is an argument to measure it the way you'd measure weather: continuously, per engine, with rates instead of readings.
