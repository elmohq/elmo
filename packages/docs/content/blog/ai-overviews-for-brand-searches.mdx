**For two years, your own brand name was the one Google search where you could count on not seeing an AI Overview. That stopped being true in September 2026.** Ahrefs measured an AI Overview on [82.91% of US desktop results for branded keywords on 29 September 2026](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-on-branded-searches/). DemandSphere, tracking a different keyword set across all markets and devices, recorded [82.06% on the same day](https://www.demandsphere.com/blog/branded-ai-overviews-september-2026/), up from 26.12% on 1 September. In September 2025, Ahrefs' figure for branded searches was [13.1%](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-triggers/).

The question an in-house team should be asking is no longer whether Google summarises your brand when people search for it. It is what that summary is written from, because in the largest sample published so far, it is mostly not your website.

**Key takeaways**

- Branded searches went from the least likely to carry an AI Overview to roughly as likely as any other query. In Ahrefs' data the gap with non-branded searches shrank from 12.2 points in July 2026 to 5 points in September.
- Bare brand names lag but are catching up fast: 5 of 98 global brands had an overview on 1 July, 63 of 100 by 30 September.
- The headline numbers disagree because they count different things. Bare names, brand-plus-modifier keywords, and intent-classified navigational queries are separate populations.
- Day-to-day presence is volatile. DemandSphere's series jumped from 28.57% to 57.43% between 16 and 18 September. One manual check of your brand SERP tells you very little.
- Wikipedia was cited in 72% of branded overviews with visible sources, YouTube in 39%, LinkedIn in 23%. Those pages now write the first paragraph a searcher reads about you.

## What changed, in six datasets

Every published measurement points the same way, but no two measure the same thing. Lined up by date:

| Source | Population | Period | AI Overview presence |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Seer Interactive](https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/case-study-analyzing-the-impact-of-ai-overviews-on-organic-search-performance) | 1,122 branded queries from client data | Published March 2025 | 4.9% (non-branded: 12.4%) |
| [Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/blog/semrush-ai-overviews-study/) | Navigational keywords from a 10M+ keyword database | Jan → Oct 2025 | 0.74% → 10.33% |
| [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-triggers/) | 54.2M branded desktop SERPs | September 2025 | 13.1% (non-branded: 24.9%) |
| [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-on-branded-searches/) | 92.5M branded US desktop crawls | July → Sept 2026, monthly averages | 61.3% → 65.1% → 73.3% |
| Ahrefs | 100 bare brand names from Interbrand's Best Global Brands | 1 July → 30 Sept 2026 | 5 of 98 → 63 of 100 |
| [DemandSphere](https://www.demandsphere.com/blog/branded-ai-overviews-september-2026/) | Branded keywords, all markets and devices, daily | 1 → 29 Sept 2026 | 26.12% → 82.06% |

Three readings fall out of the table.

**The shift is real and recent.** Through 2025, every dataset put branded or navigational presence somewhere between under 1% and 13%, and where non-branded was measured alongside, at roughly half its rate. By the end of September 2026, two independent panels with different keywords, markets, and devices landed within a point of each other at around 82%. Search Engine Roundtable's Barry Schwartz [tested dozens of brands in late 2024 and found none](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-ai-overviews-for-branded-navigational-queries-38122.html) carrying an overview; by 30 September 2026 he was [documenting them on Amazon, Apple, Samsung, Reddit, and Adobe](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-ai-overviews-large-brand-names-42195.html). Google has not announced the change.

**"Branded" is three different things.** A bare brand name ("adobe") is the purest navigational query: the searcher wants the site. A brand plus a modifier ("adobe pricing", "adobe vs canva") is often an informational or commercial question that happens to contain a name, and those were triggering overviews long before the bare name did. That is why Ahrefs' bare-name panel (63%) sits below its branded-keyword figure (83%) at the same moment, and why Semrush's intent-classified navigational number (10.33% in October 2025) is not comparable to either. When someone quotes you a percentage, ask which of the three it counts. Ahrefs' article does not say how it classified a keyword as branded, which matters for anyone trying to reproduce it.

**Snapshots mislead.** DemandSphere's daily series is the most useful thing published on this, because it shows the rollout was not a smooth ramp. Presence sat near 27% for the first week of September, then bounced: 28.57% on 16 September, 57.43% on 18 September, 31.15% on 19 September, 90.48% on 27 September. A team that checked its brand SERP once on the 19th would have concluded nothing had changed. Monthly averages flatten this in the other direction: Ahrefs' 73.3% September average understates where the month finished.

## What Google writes your brand summary from

The sources matter more than the rate, and this is the part with the thinnest evidence. Ahrefs examined the 57 branded overviews in its panel that showed a source list:

| Cited domain | Overviews citing it | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Wikipedia | 41 | 71.9% |
| YouTube | 22 | 38.6% |
| LinkedIn | 13 | 22.8% |
| Yahoo Finance | 9 | 15.8% |
| Facebook | 6 | 10.5% |
| Walmart | 4 | 7.0% |

Two caveats the source mentions only in passing. First, the panel is 100 of the largest brands in the world, nearly all of which have a substantial Wikipedia article and a Yahoo Finance listing. A mid-market SaaS company without a Wikipedia page will be summarised from something else, and nobody has published what. Second, 57 overviews is a small sample for a source breakdown; read the ranking, not the decimals.

Even so, the direction matches what we see in non-branded AI answers: [most citations come from places other than your own site](/blog/where-ai-citations-come-from), and Google's AI surfaces lean on [YouTube](/blog/youtube-ai-citations) far more than other engines do. [LinkedIn's appearance](/blog/linkedin-ai-citations) is the one to look at closely. For branded queries the cited page is plausibly the company page itself, which would be unusual: across general AI answers, most LinkedIn citations go to individual members rather than company pages. Ahrefs does not say which it found.

The practical consequence is that the third-party pages you have ignored for years are now quoted directly above your homepage. A video cited in your brand overview may not come from your official channel. A Wikipedia article last edited two product generations ago becomes the description. And [AI engines do not reliably fact-check what they read about you](/blog/ai-search-fact-checking) before repeating it.

## What has not changed yet

Competitors are mostly absent. Of 58 branded overviews whose full text Ahrefs could read, 2 (3.4%) discussed competitors, and only one competitor-owned site appeared among the 57 source lists. Treat that as a description of September 2026, not a guarantee. A branded overview that says "popular alternatives include..." is a small change for Google and a large one for you, and it is exactly the kind of drift that a one-off audit will miss.

Placement is unsettled too. Ahrefs found 48 of its 63 bare-name overviews (76.2%) in the first position on the page. Schwartz's screenshots from the same week showed most of them mid-page, with Adobe the exception. Both can be accurate if placement varies by brand and by day, which the DemandSphere volatility suggests. Placement decides how much of your branded click the overview takes, so it is worth recording for your own name rather than assuming either answer.

And Google's own name and news publishers [reportedly still do not get one](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-ai-overviews-large-brand-names-42195.html).

## A decision rule for your brand

Not every brand needs to act on this immediately. Use the cited sources to decide:

1. **No overview on your bare name after a week of checks.** Monitor monthly. You are in the 37% that had none on 30 September, and that share is shrinking.
2. **An overview citing your own site and accurate third-party pages.** No action beyond monitoring. This is the good outcome.
3. **An overview citing a third-party page with wrong or outdated facts.** Fix that page first. It outranks any change to your own site, because Google is quoting it, not you.
4. **An overview citing videos or social posts you do not control.** Publish a current, official equivalent and make sure it is easy to identify as yours. You cannot remove the other source; you can give Google a better one.
5. **An overview that names a competitor.** Track the query daily and record what it says. This was rare in September 2026; if it is happening to you, you are early, and you want the history.

Opting out is not on the list. Google's [AI Overviews opt-out](/blog/opt-out-google-ai-overviews) removes your pages as a source; it does not stop the summary being generated. On a branded query, that hands the description of your company entirely to Wikipedia, YouTube, and LinkedIn.

## Measuring it

Your branded queries belong in your [tracked prompt set](/blog/ai-prompt-tracking), split by bare name and modifiers, and run often enough to see through daily noise. Search Console will not do this for you: it [reports clicks and impressions, not whether an overview appeared or what it said](/blog/search-console-ai-visibility). The metrics that matter are the ones for any AI answer: whether an overview appears, which domains it cites, whether your site is among them, and [how it describes you](/blog/ai-brand-sentiment).

[Elmo](https://www.elmohq.com/) is an open-source, self-hosted AI visibility platform that runs your prompts across Google AI Overviews, AI Mode, ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and Perplexity on a schedule, recording every mention, cited source, and competitor named alongside you. Add your brand name and its modifiers as prompts, and the cited-source breakdown above becomes a weekly report on your own name instead of a 57-overview sample of someone else's.
