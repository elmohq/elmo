**Every AI visibility tool is built on top of a scraping API, and which one it sits on decides what the numbers mean.** ChatGPT, Google AI Mode, and Gemini have no public API for the interface real users see, so tracking what an answer engine says about your brand means driving the product itself. We build [Elmo](https://github.com/elmohq/elmo), an open-source AI visibility tracker, and we run six of these scraping providers side by side in production. This is what we've learned about the trade-offs.

**Key takeaways**

- Entry pricing spans about 5x for identical work. Tracking one prompt across ChatGPT and Google AI Mode for a month costs roughly $0.45 on [Oxylabs](https://oxylabs.go2cloud.org/aff_c?offer_id=7&aff_id=2263&url_id=32) or [BrightData](https://get.brightdata.com/elmo?sid=blog) and $2.15 on [Olostep](https://olostep.com/?ref=elmo)'s smallest plan.
- Headline per-run pricing is the wrong number to compare. If you're only tracking a handful of prompts, a free tier or a provider without a monthly minimum might be better. At scale, volume pricing reshuffles the order: [SearchApi](https://www.searchapi.io/?via=elmo)'s largest plan is the cheapest per run here.
- Coverage is not uniform. [Cloro](https://cloro.dev?fpr=elmo&fp_sid=blog), [SearchApi](https://www.searchapi.io/?via=elmo), [Olostep](https://olostep.com/?ref=elmo), [DataForSEO](https://try.dataforseo.com/elmo), and [BrightData](https://get.brightdata.com/elmo?sid=blog) reach all four surfaces; Elmo doesn't use [Oxylabs](https://oxylabs.go2cloud.org/aff_c?offer_id=7&aff_id=2263&url_id=32)' Gemini scraper yet. Perplexity and the Copilot app now sit behind a sign-in, so no provider scrapes either; Bing AI Mode is the Microsoft surface left, through Cloro or SearchApi.
- Reliability varies more than price does, and it is the variable nobody publishes. A failed scrape is a hole in your prompt's history that you cannot backfill. In October, success rates ranged from 97% to 61%.
- Google AI Overview is where providers split. [SearchApi](https://www.searchapi.io/?via=elmo), [Olostep](https://olostep.com/?ref=elmo), and [Cloro](https://cloro.dev?fpr=elmo&fp_sid=blog) all cleared 97% on it; the other three were at 58% or below.
- Median latency ranges from 11 seconds to over a minute, and individual runs took up to nearly 20 minutes. It rarely matters for scheduled tracking but matters enormously for anything a user is waiting on.

## What an LLM scraping API actually does

An LLM scraping API takes a prompt, drives a consumer AI product with it, and returns the generated answer as structured JSON, usually with the cited source URLs and sometimes the search queries the product ran behind the scenes.

That last part is why the category exists at all. You can approximate ChatGPT by calling `gpt-5-mini` with web search enabled, but it is significantly more expensive and the results will be different. This is because the ChatGPT product wraps the model in its own retrieval, ranking, and formatting layers, and those layers are what decide whether your brand gets named and which sources get linked. The model API answer and the product answer routinely disagree about both.

The same holds for Google. AI Mode and AI Overviews are Search features, not models, and there is no endpoint that returns them.

Two things this table hides, both of which change the answer at small scale.

**Plan minimums beat unit prices.** [Oxylabs](https://oxylabs.go2cloud.org/aff_c?offer_id=7&aff_id=2263&url_id=32) has no pay-as-you-go option, so the entry price is its $30/mo [Starter plan](https://oxylabs.io/pricing/web-scraper-api), which covers about 20,000 LLM scraper results, or around 66 tracked prompts. If you are tracking ten prompts, you are paying $30 for $4.50 of scraping. [BrightData](https://get.brightdata.com/elmo?sid=blog) bills per record with no monthly commitment, and its free 5,000 records a month cover about 16 prompts on this workload. **Credits are not requests.** [Cloro prices per credit](https://cloro.dev/pricing/), and each surface costs a different number: Gemini, Google AI Mode, and Bing AI Mode 4, Google AI Overview 5, ChatGPT 7. One prompt on ChatGPT plus Google AI Mode is 11 credits a day, or about 1,650 a month. The $30 Lite plan's 37,500 credits therefore covers about 22 prompts, at about $1.30 each; the rate halves to $0.65 from the $100 Hobby plan up. [Olostep](https://olostep.com/?ref=elmo) works the same way, at 5 credits for ChatGPT and 3 for every other surface, so its $9 Starter plan's 5,000 credits cover about four prompts.

## The variable nobody publishes: reliability

Price and coverage are on every vendor's website. Success rate is not, and it is the number that actually determines whether your tracking data is usable.

A failed scrape is not the same kind of problem as a slow one. Retries may cost money and eventually give up, and a run that never completes leaves a gap in a time series that cannot be backfilled, because the answer that surface would have given yesterday no longer exists.

## The six providers

### Cloro (Recommended)

The most consistent provider across every surface we ran in October: 97% overall, and at least 97% everywhere except Copilot, where it managed 92%. It has good documentation and an API that is pleasant to integrate against. Requests go through an [asynchronous task API](https://cloro.dev/docs/) that you poll, which also lets Cloro meter concurrency rather than holding a connection open. Failed requests are not charged.

What sets Cloro apart is that it publishes [uptime for each engine](https://status.cloro.dev/). Most scraping providers' status pages show one green dot for the whole API. That dot tells you nothing when ChatGPT scraping is broken and Google AI Mode is fine, and that is exactly how these scrapers fail. Cloro's status page tracks ChatGPT, Gemini, Google AI Mode, Google AI Overview, and Bing AI Mode separately, with incident history for each, so you can tell at a glance whether a failure is theirs or yours. It is the same data we collect for every provider on our own [status page](https://www.elmohq.com/status), published by the provider itself.

This level of transparency should be the standard, not the exception. Scraping AI answer engines means chasing UIs that change without notice, so every provider breaks sometimes. The real question is whether a provider tells you when it happens. Cloro does, engine by engine, and we'd like every provider on this list to follow.

It is plan-based rather than pay-as-you-go, and the $30 Lite plan works out to about $1.30 per prompt, so it stings on very small projects. From the $100 Hobby plan up, the rate halves to about $0.65. There is also a free tier of 500 credits a month for testing.

It is also the provider we put our own money behind: [Cloro](https://cloro.dev?fpr=elmo&fp_sid=blog) is the primary data source for Elmo Cloud, so the numbers in the table above are the same ones our paying customers depend on. Ahrefs, Scrunch, and Evertune run on it too.

### SearchApi

Matched [Cloro](https://cloro.dev?fpr=elmo&fp_sid=blog) at 97% in October and was the fastest provider we run, with an 11-second median and no successful run over 1m28s. It was perfect on Google AI Mode, Google AI Overview, and Copilot; ChatGPT was its weakest surface at 92%.

It is also one of the simplest integrations here. One bearer API key and one synchronous endpoint serve every surface, selected by an `engine` parameter, so each run is a single request with no task queue to poll. The documentation is good, and ChatGPT results report whether the model searched the web.

[Pricing](https://www.searchapi.io/pricing) is flat per search regardless of surface, and you only pay for successful searches. The exception is Google AI Overview, which can take a second search when Google doesn't return the overview inline. At $4 per 1,000 searches on the $40/mo Developer plan, it costs about $1.20 per prompt, slightly less than [Cloro](https://cloro.dev?fpr=elmo&fp_sid=blog)'s entry plan; on the largest plans it falls to $1 per 1,000, the cheapest per run of any provider here. There is no pay-as-you-go tier, plans cap usage at 20% of your monthly searches in any hour, and sign-up includes 100 free requests.

<Callout title="Best for">Teams that want fast, synchronous results and predictable per-search pricing that gets cheaper at volume.</Callout>

### Olostep

The provider behind Profound, Athena, and AirOps, and genuinely good at high-volume scraping with a well-designed API. It covers all four surfaces and hit 96% in October, including 99% on ChatGPT and Google AI Overview.

[Pricing](https://www.olostep.com/pricing) is credit-based, from a $9/mo Starter plan that covers only about four prompts at $2.15 each. The per-credit rate drops sharply on larger plans: the $99/mo Standard plan works out to about $0.60 per prompt.

Its 32-second median is competitive, but the tail is long. Individual runs approached 20 minutes, the slowest of any provider we run, which rules it out for anything a user is waiting on.

<Callout title="Best for">Large-scale batch scraping where throughput matters more than the slowest individual run.</Callout>

<ProviderCTA href="https://olostep.com/?ref=elmo" name="Olostep" />

### DataForSEO

The most flexible of the six, and the only one that fronts both scrapers and direct model APIs behind a single account. ChatGPT and Gemini come from its [LLM Scraper](https://docs.dataforseo.com/v3/ai_optimization-chat_gpt-llm_scraper-live-advanced/) and the two Google surfaces from SERP endpoints.

ChatGPT and Gemini were near-perfect in October at 98% and 99%, with an overall median of 19 seconds. Google AI Overview pulled it down to 85% overall: only 58% of those runs succeeded, most failing with an internal error from DataForSEO's search backend.

Pay-as-you-go with a $50 minimum top-up and no subscription. Its queues give you a real cost lever: the standard queue costs less than a third of live mode, about $0.36 per prompt, if you can wait up to 45 minutes for results.

<Callout title="Best for">Anyone who wants pay-as-you-go pricing and the option to mix scraped and model-API data without a second vendor.</Callout>

<ProviderCTA href="https://try.dataforseo.com/elmo" name="DataForSEO" />

### Oxylabs

Tied with [BrightData](https://get.brightdata.com/elmo?sid=blog) for the cheapest entry price, at about $0.45 per prompt. Oxylabs sells one subscription across all its products, from the $30/mo Starter plan, and new accounts get $5 of free credits. Requests go through an asynchronous Push-Pull API that avoids the connection timeouts of their realtime endpoint.

Three real limitations. Its Gemini scraper is new enough that Elmo doesn't support it yet. There is no pay-as-you-go tier. And it was the second-least reliable provider we ran in October at 69%, with ChatGPT at 82% and Google AI Overview at 26%, where most jobs faulted on Oxylabs' side.

<Callout title="Best for">High volume on ChatGPT and Google AI Mode, where the per-run saving compounds and you can tolerate gaps.</Callout>

<ProviderCTA href="https://oxylabs.go2cloud.org/aff_c?offer_id=7&aff_id=2263&url_id=32" name="Oxylabs" />

### BrightData

A common default for web scraping in general. Pay-as-you-go at $1.50 per 1,000 records with no monthly commitment, tied with [Oxylabs](https://oxylabs.go2cloud.org/aff_c?offer_id=7&aff_id=2263&url_id=32) for the cheapest entry price, and a free tier of 5,000 records a month, which covers about 16 prompts on ChatGPT and Google AI Mode.

October was rough. It was the least reliable provider we ran at 61% and the slowest, with a 65-second median. ChatGPT and Gemini held up at 89% and 88%, but Google AI Mode fell to 71% and Google AI Overview to 28%. Copilot didn't return a single successful run: requests first hit a sign-in wall, then BrightData's dataset began rejecting collection requests entirely. Copilot has since gone behind a sign-in for every provider.

<Callout title="Best for">Trying out ChatGPT and Gemini scraping on the free tier before committing to a plan.</Callout>

## How to choose

- **What's the best overall?** [Cloro](https://cloro.dev?fpr=elmo&fp_sid=blog). It covers everything at a reasonable price and rarely fails. It's what we use for Elmo Cloud.
- **Need speed, or a low per-run price at volume?** [SearchApi](https://www.searchapi.io/?via=elmo). It matched Cloro's reliability with the fastest median we measured, and its largest plans are the cheapest per run here.
- **Only tracking a handful of prompts?** [BrightData](https://get.brightdata.com/elmo?sid=blog)'s free tier covers about 16 prompts a month, and [DataForSEO](https://try.dataforseo.com/elmo)'s pay-as-you-go balance never expires.
- **Need Copilot?** The Copilot app requires a sign-in now, so track Bing AI Mode instead through [Cloro](https://cloro.dev?fpr=elmo&fp_sid=blog) or [SearchApi](https://www.searchapi.io/?via=elmo).
- **Need Perplexity?** No scraper reaches it now that it requires a sign-in. Track it through its Sonar model on OpenRouter or [DataForSEO](https://try.dataforseo.com/elmo) instead.
- **Need Google AI Overview?** Use [SearchApi](https://www.searchapi.io/?via=elmo), [Olostep](https://olostep.com/?ref=elmo), or [Cloro](https://cloro.dev?fpr=elmo&fp_sid=blog). Everyone else was at 58% or below.

## How we measured this

Elmo is open source, and the [provider integrations](https://github.com/elmohq/elmo/tree/main/packages/lib/src/providers/registry) are readable if you want to see exactly what each request does. Coverage in the table above is taken from those integrations rather than from vendor marketing.

The reliability and latency figures come from the same store that backs Elmo's [status page](https://www.elmohq.com/status), which runs real prompts against every provider and surface several times a day and records every result, pass or fail. The tables above cover 2,700 runs across 30 targets from October 5 to October 31, 2026, de-duplicated so that retries within five minutes count once, which is the same aggregation the status page renders. Success rate is passing runs over total runs; latency is measured on passing runs only, since a failure's latency is time-to-error.

Pricing is taken from each vendor's public pricing pages as of November 2026 and normalised to the same workload. Vendors change prices; check before you commit.
