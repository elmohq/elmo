**Yes, there is an open-source alternative to Profound, Peec AI, and OtterlyAI.** [Elmo](https://www.elmohq.com/) is an MIT-licensed AI visibility platform that does the job all three are mostly bought for: run your buyers' prompts through the answer engines on a schedule, then track whether you're mentioned, who is cited, how you compare to competitors, and how that changes over time. You can self-host it for free or use Elmo Cloud from $29/mo, and every metric is computed by code you can read.

This post is published by Elmo, so we are an interested party. Every price and engine claim below was checked against each vendor's own site in November 2026, and every open-source project links to its public repository so you can check it yourself.

**Key takeaways**

- Profound, Peec AI, and OtterlyAI are closed, hosted subscriptions. Profound is custom Enterprise pricing after a 7-day trial, Peec AI starts at $95/mo, and OtterlyAI starts at $29/mo for 15 prompts.
- Elmo replaces the core of all three: mention tracking, citations, competitor share of voice, sentiment, and query fan-out across ChatGPT, Google AI Mode, Google AI Overviews, Gemini, Copilot, Claude, and Grok.
- Other open-source options exist (Canonry and OneGlanse), and each makes a real tradeoff. Both started in 2026.
- No open-source tool replaces prompt-volume estimates, shopping tracking, geographic breakdowns, or content generation.
- Free to self-host is not free to run. You pay for model or scraper API calls and the infrastructure.

## Which Open-Source Tool Replaces Each One

**Replacing Profound.** Teams leave Profound mostly over price and weight: a custom Enterprise contract and a suite that now spans agents, content, shopping, and ads. Elmo does the measurement part, meaning visibility, share of voice, citations, query fan-out, and ranked recommendations, with a REST API, TypeScript and Python SDKs, and an MCP server. It does not do prompt volumes, crawler analytics, shopping, or content agents.

**Replacing Peec AI.** Peec AI starts at $95/mo, its self-serve plans pick three of six engines, and Claude and Grok are Enterprise-only. Self-hosted Elmo has no engine gating and no prompt cap; you pay your API provider per run instead. On Elmo Cloud, the $99/mo Basic plan picks any four engines.

**Replacing OtterlyAI.** OtterlyAI is the cheapest of the three, but its $29 plan is 15 prompts, and Gemini, AI Mode, and Claude cost extra. Elmo Cloud's $29 Starter plan covers 50 prompts on ChatGPT, and self-hosted Elmo covers every engine with no add-ons.

For a team replacing a commercial subscription, Elmo is the closest match: it has the dashboard, the multi-engine coverage, and the reporting a marketing team expects from Profound, Peec AI, or OtterlyAI, plus an open-source cloud if you don't want to run it.

## What Open Source Doesn't Replace

Be clear about the gaps before you switch.

- **Prompt volumes.** Profound, Peec AI, and OtterlyAI estimate how often a prompt is asked using proprietary data. No open-source tool can do that, because the data isn't public.
- **Shopping and geography.** Product-carousel tracking and per-country breakdowns are commercial-only today.
- **Content generation.** The commercial suites increasingly write and publish content. Elmo tells you what to change and leaves the writing to you.

If those features are why you bought the tool, an open-source tracker will not replace it. If you bought it to know whether AI answers mention and cite you, it will.

## What Self-Hosting Actually Costs

The software is free under the MIT license. Running it is not. Measuring AI visibility means sending real prompts to real engines, so you pay your scraper or model API provider for every run, plus a small server and a PostgreSQL database. You also own upgrades.

In exchange you get unlimited prompts and engines, your prompts and history stay in your own database, and you can read exactly how each number was produced. If you'd rather not run anything, [Elmo Cloud](/pricing) runs the same code, and you can move between the two at any time.
