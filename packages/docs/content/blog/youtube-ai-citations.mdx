**There are at least six published numbers for how often AI answers cite YouTube, and they span 0.99% to 23.3%.** Surfer counted 46 million AI Overview citations and put YouTube first at [about 23.3%](https://surferseo.com/blog/ai-citation-report/). Meltwater counted 9.5 million citations across six platforms and put YouTube first at [1.52%](https://www.meltwater.com/en/blog/linkedin-ai-visibility-study). Profound data reported by [Marketing Dive](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/behind-reddit-and-youtubes-roles-in-ai-visibility/827313/) put it at 0.99% of ChatGPT citations — not first at all. Every figure is sound. The spread is engines and denominators, and sorting it out is the difference between a video strategy and a video mistake.

**Key takeaways**

- On Google's AI surfaces, YouTube is the top-cited domain in every dataset that isolates them: **23.3%** of citations in Surfer's March–August 2025 corpus, **21.1%** of top-source citations in Ahrefs' July 2026 tracking.
- Across chat assistants it is a top source in a field with no dominant sources: Meltwater's **1.52%** ranks first among all domains while remaining a rounding error of total citations.
- ChatGPT is the exception that decides budgets: **0.99%** of citations, still well behind Reddit's 2.4%.
- The widely repeated "YouTube overtook Reddit" finding — Bluefish's [16% of LLM answers versus 10%](https://www.adweek.com/media/youtube-reddit-ai-search-engine-citations/) — is real, engine-dependent, and measured on a different denominator than any citation-share figure.
- Videos get cited without ranking: among AI Overview citations that ranked nowhere in Google's top 100, [18.2% were YouTube URLs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-citations-top-10/), the largest single group.
- No published study splits brand channels from creator channels. The data says video gets cited; it does not say *your* videos get cited.

## Six numbers, three denominators

Set the published figures side by side and the range looks like a contradiction. It is mostly arithmetic.

| Study | Surface | Metric | YouTube | Reddit, same study |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Surfer](https://surferseo.com/blog/ai-citation-report/), Mar–Aug 2025, 46M citations | AI Overviews | Share of citations | 23.3% | — |
| [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/most-cited-domains-ai-overviews/), Jul 2026, 3M+ US queries | AI Overviews | Share of top-50 sources' citations | 21.1% | 18.5% |
| [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-citations-top-10/), Mar 2026, 863k SERPs | AI Overviews | Share of all citations | 5.6% | — |
| Bluefish via [Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/media/youtube-reddit-ai-search-engine-citations/), Jan 2026 | LLM assistants, six months | Response citation rate | 16% of answers | 10% |
| [Meltwater](https://www.meltwater.com/en/blog/linkedin-ai-visibility-study), 9.5M citations | Six platforms | Share of all citations | 1.52% | — |
| Profound via [Marketing Dive](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/behind-reddit-and-youtubes-roles-in-ai-visibility/827313/) | ChatGPT | Share of citations | 0.99% | 2.4% |

Three things generate the spread. **The surface**: Google's AI answers cite YouTube at a different order of magnitude than the assistants do. **The denominator**: Ahrefs' 21.1% is YouTube's slice of the citations earned by the top 50 domains only — its own methodology note says mention share is "a domain's citations as a percentage of the summed citations of the top 50 most-cited sources" — while its March study's 5.6% is a share of *all* citations, long tail included. Same company, same surface, both correct. **The metric**: Bluefish's 16% counts answers containing at least one YouTube link, which is the same response-versus-citation split that produced [LinkedIn's 11%-versus-0.53% pair](/blog/linkedin-ai-citations).

The number relevant to a visibility decision is the one matching your engine and your question. "How much of the AI Overviews citation pool is video?" is the 5.6%. "Who wins the head-to-head among top sources there?" is the 21.1%. "How often does an assistant answer contain a video?" is the 16%.

## On Google's surfaces, YouTube is the house source

Whatever else the datasets disagree on, they agree here. Surfer's corpus — 36 million AI Overviews between March and August 2025 — has YouTube first at 23.3%, ahead of Wikipedia at 18.4%. Ahrefs' July 2026 tracking has it first at 21.1%, ahead of Reddit at 18.5%. And [LQ Digital's comparison](https://www.pr.com/press-release/972573) of 8,000-plus citations against organic results for the same queries found YouTube appearing **4.3x more often in AI Overviews than in organic** — the mirror image of Reddit, which runs 3.9x the other way, a pattern covered in our review of [Reddit's AI citations](/blog/reddit-ai-citations).

Three explanations fit, and none excludes the others. The first is ownership. Google cites its own video platform at rates no other engine matches, and Surfer's same dataset has google.com itself at 16.4% of citations — the third most-cited domain in Google's own answers is Google. Nobody outside Google can measure intent here, but the benchmark consequence is measurable either way: YouTube's dominance of AI Overviews tells you nothing about engines Google does not run.

The second is fan-out. Ahrefs' March 2026 data found that among cited pages ranking nowhere in Google's top 100 for the query, 18.2% were YouTube URLs. A system that [retrieves against generated sub-queries rather than the typed keyword](/blog/rankings-and-ai-citations) will surface a video that text rankings buried, because the video answers a sub-question no ranked page covers.

The third is extraction. The line from Adweek's report is the mechanism in one sentence: transcripts, explainers, and the text around videos "have allowed the video platform to flourish as a source that machines can easily read." An engine does not watch a video. It reads the transcript, the chapters, and the description — which means the citable unit is the spoken words, and a video whose substance lives on screen rather than in the audio is invisible at retrieval time.

The vertical spread is wide enough to check before acting on any average. Surfer's per-industry figures run from roughly 23% of citations in finance to 32% in e-commerce and 39% in SEO topics, with gaming at an extreme it reports as over 90%. Your category's number is knowable from your own [tracked prompts](/blog/ai-prompt-tracking) and is the only one that should move budget.

## On the assistants, a top source among no dominant sources

Meltwater's 9.5 million citations across six platforms rank YouTube first among all domains — at 1.52% of citations. That is what first place looks like on the open-web assistants: [no domain holds a meaningful share](/blog/where-ai-citations-come-from), and the leader clears the field at one and a half percent.

The Bluefish finding sits on top of this. YouTube in 16% of LLM answers against Reddit's 10%, over six months, reversed the ordering that the entire Reddit-for-AEO thesis was built on. Adweek's report attributes the rise to machine-readable transcripts, though it publishes no engine-level breakdown or methodology, so the number is best read as a direction rather than a benchmark.

ChatGPT is where the direction fails. Profound's figures give Reddit 2.4% of ChatGPT citations and YouTube 0.99% — the one major engine where the overtake has not happened, and the engine with the largest user base. A team that reads "YouTube overtook Reddit" and shifts effort accordingly, while its buyers ask in ChatGPT, has optimized for the wrong machine.

## What this does not prove

**All of it is vendor research.** Surfer, Ahrefs, and Meltwater sell tooling adjacent to the conclusion; Bluefish and Profound sell AI visibility services and published through trade press without full methodology. Surfer's report states no limitations at all. Ahrefs' most-cited list is regenerated monthly by its own automated agent, which keeps it current and makes any quoted figure a snapshot. The direction of the findings survives this; the second decimal place does not.

**Cited is not mentioned, and neither is recommended.** A YouTube citation under an answer does not mean the answer named your brand — the [ghost citation](/blog/ghost-citations) gap applies to video sources the same as any other. And a citation to a comparison video naming four competitors is not obviously a win.

**Nobody has split brand channels from creator channels.** Every study counts youtube.com as one domain. Whether the cited videos in your category come from brands' own channels or from independent creators is the decision-relevant split — it is the difference between a production budget and a creator-relations program, the same way [LinkedIn's citations split between profiles and company pages](/blog/linkedin-ai-citations) — and no published dataset answers it. Your own cited-URL data does.

**The numbers move.** Bluefish's measurement is a six-month window ending January 2026; Surfer's corpus ends August 2025; Ahrefs updates monthly. Given [how fast cited sources turn over](/blog/citation-volatility), treat every figure here as a reading, not a constant.

## What to do with it

1. **Pull the cited YouTube URLs from your own tracked prompts, per engine.** The published spread — 21% on Google's surfaces, 1% on ChatGPT — is wide enough that no cross-engine average supports a decision.
2. **Look at the channel names in those URLs.** Brand channels mean you should publish; creator channels mean you should brief. No study makes this call for you.
3. **If AI Overviews carry your category, treat video as underweighted.** YouTube appears 4.3x more often there than in organic results for the same queries; most content plans still allocate as if organic were the whole game.
4. **Write for the transcript.** Say the answer out loud, in extractable sentences, near the start. Upload a clean transcript, add chapters, and put specifics in the description — the engine reads text, and auto-captions are the worst text you can hand it.
5. **Do not grade citation-earning videos on views.** Retrieval and recommendation are different systems; a video can feed answers for months while looking dead in YouTube Studio.
6. **Keep Reddit and everything else in the mix on ChatGPT.** The overtake headline is engine-specific, and on the highest-volume assistant the old ordering still holds.
