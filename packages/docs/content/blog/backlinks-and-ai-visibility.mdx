**Your link-building budget was justified by a metric AI answer engines show no sign of reading.** Three correlation studies published across 2025 — [Ahrefs on 75,000 brands](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-brand-correlation/), [Seer Interactive on 10,000 buyer questions](https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/what-drives-brand-mentions-in-ai-answers), and [Semrush with Kevin Indig on 1,000 domains](https://www.semrush.com/blog/backlinks-ai-search-study/) — asked what predicts whether a brand shows up in AI answers, and none of them found raw backlink counts near the top. Two found them near the bottom. The third looks like a contradiction — domain authority at a 0.65 correlation with AI mentions — until you notice the detail that reconciles all three: nofollow links worked exactly as well as followed ones. Engines are not consuming link equity. They are reading links as mentions.

**Key facts**

| | |
|---|---|
| Branded web mentions vs AI Overview presence | **0.664** Spearman — [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-brand-correlation/), 75,000 brands, May 2025 |
| Backlinks vs AI Overview presence, same dataset | **0.218** Spearman |
| Backlink impact on ChatGPT mentions | **"weak or even neutral"** — [Seer Interactive](https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/what-drives-brand-mentions-in-ai-answers), 10,000 questions, Jan 2025 |
| Page-1 Google rankings vs ChatGPT mentions, same dataset | **~0.65** |
| Authority Score vs AI mentions | **0.65** Pearson — [Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/blog/backlinks-ai-search-study/), 1,000 domains, Oct 2025 |
| Nofollow vs followed links against AI mentions | **0.509 vs 0.504** Spearman, same dataset |
| Top quartile of brands by web mentions vs next quartile | **169 vs 14** average AI Overview mentions — Ahrefs |

**Key takeaways**

- Raw backlink volume is one of the weakest measured predictors of AI visibility: 0.218 in Ahrefs' data, below branded search volume, domain rating, and even ad spend.
- Branded web mentions are the strongest measured off-site factor at 0.664, and the distribution is brutal: Ahrefs' top mention quartile averaged 169 AI Overview mentions, the next quartile 14, and the bottom half was close to invisible.
- The Semrush finding that looks like a rebuttal — Authority Score at 0.65 Pearson against AI mentions — measures a composite that includes organic traffic, and its gains cluster above authority thresholds rather than accruing per link.
- The tell is nofollow: 0.509 versus 0.504 for followed links. Whatever AI systems do with a link, it is not the equity calculation that justified followed-link pricing for twenty years.
- The three studies measured three different pipelines — Google's AI Overviews, an ungrounded GPT‑4o, and five engines blended — and agree anyway: what predicts presence is being named and being retrievable, not being linked.
- The practical move is not cutting the budget but changing its KPI: from followed links and anchors to brand-naming placements on pages engines actually retrieve.

## What did the three studies actually measure?

The three datasets are independent — different vendors, different samples, different engines, different years of publication — which is what makes their agreement worth something.

| Study | Sample | Engine(s) | Backlink signal | Strongest signal found |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-brand-correlation/), May 2025 | 75,000 brands (DR > 40, ≥ 800 monthly branded searches) | Google AI Overviews | Backlinks **0.218**, referring domains 0.295 | Branded web mentions **0.664** |
| [Seer Interactive](https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/what-drives-brand-mentions-in-ai-answers), Jan 2025 | 10,000 questions distilled from 300,000+ finance and SaaS keywords | GPT‑4o via API | **"Weak or even neutral"** | Page-1 Google rankings **~0.65** |
| [Semrush × Kevin Indig](https://www.semrush.com/blog/backlinks-ai-search-study/), Oct 2025 | 1,000 randomly selected domains | ChatGPT, ChatGPT Search, Gemini, AI Overviews, Perplexity | Follow links 0.504 Spearman | Authority Score **0.65** Pearson |

Ahrefs ran Spearman correlations between eleven site metrics and how often each brand appeared in AI Overviews. The ranking of factors is the finding: branded web mentions (0.664), branded anchors (0.527), branded search volume (0.392), then Domain Rating (0.326), referring domains (0.295) — and backlinks at 0.218, barely ahead of ad traffic (0.216). The quartile view is starker than the coefficients: the top 25% of brands by web mentions averaged 169 AI Overview mentions, the next quartile 14, more than a 10x cliff, with the bottom half essentially absent.

Seer approached it from the buyer's side: 300,000+ finance and SaaS keywords narrowed to 10,000 real question-style prompts, run through OpenAI's GPT‑4o API, with the resulting brand mentions correlated against search-market data. Backlinks, which the authors say they expected to matter, came back "weak or even neutral." What did correlate was ranking on page 1 of Google (~0.65) and, less strongly, on Bing (~0.5–0.6).

Semrush and Kevin Indig took 1,000 random domains across five engines and went deeper into link *composition*: follow versus nofollow, text versus image, volume versus authority. Their headline is that Authority Score correlates with AI mentions at 0.65 Pearson — and that most link metrics only pay off above authority thresholds, visible in the gaps between their Pearson and Spearman figures.

## Why does one study say links matter and two say they don't?

Because "backlinks" is doing three different jobs in these sentences, and only one of them is dead.

**Raw link volume is the dead one.** That is the 0.218, the "weak or even neutral," and — inside Semrush's own data — the finding that quality beats quantity everywhere they looked. If your program's KPI is the count of referring domains, all three studies say that number is close to noise for AI answers.

**Authority is alive but is not a link count.** Semrush's 0.65 is measured on Authority Score, a composite that folds organic traffic in alongside link quality. A domain with a high score is, almost by definition, a site people visit and talk about — so part of that correlation restates the Ahrefs mention finding rather than contradicting it. And the threshold behaviour cuts the other way for most teams: if gains concentrate above an authority level, the marginal link that moves a mid-sized brand's score from 42 to 44 buys little.

**Rankings are alive, and links feed rankings.** Seer's strongest factor was page-1 presence at ~0.65 — consistent with retrieval-based engines drawing on search indexes, and with [our reading of the rank–citation overlap studies](/blog/rankings-and-ai-citations): rank is a real but weakening pathway into AI answers. Links that earn rankings still earn the visibility that flows through that pathway. What they do not do is anything *additional* that the engines reward as links.

Put the three together and the reconciliation is one sentence: links correlate with AI visibility exactly to the extent that they travel with mentions, authority, and rankings — and contribute nothing measurable on their own.

## What does the nofollow result reveal?

The single most diagnostic number in any of the three datasets is the smallest gap: nofollow links correlated with AI mentions at 0.509 Spearman, followed links at 0.504. Statistically identical.

The follow/nofollow distinction exists for exactly one purpose: telling a search engine whether to pass link equity. Twenty years of link-building economics — the price gap between a followed editorial link and a nofollow directory listing — rests on that attribute. If AI visibility responded to link equity, followed links would have to outperform. They do not, at all.

What treats a followed and a nofollow link identically? A system that reads the page as text. To a model — in pretraining or in a retrieved document — both are a sentence on someone else's site that names you, describes you, and situates you in a category. The link is a mention with an address attached. The same logic explains why Semrush found *image* links slightly outperforming text links (0.538 vs 0.472 Spearman): whatever distinguishes pages that embed your screenshots and product shots, it is not equity flow.

This is the mechanism that unifies all three studies. Engines surface brands they know about (mentions in training data) and brands on the pages they retrieve (mentions in ranked, crawlable documents). Links predict visibility when and because they are mentions; the attribute layer that made them a separate asset class is unread. It is also why mention and citation come apart so often in practice — [61.7% of brand appearances in AI answers are citations with no brand mention](/blog/ghost-citations) — the engine is quoting text, and what the text says about you is the whole game.

## What do all three studies gloss over?

**Correlation, causation, and the brand-size confound.** Ahrefs says this itself: all its correlations are "moderate to very weak," and big brands accumulate mentions, links, search volume, and AI presence simultaneously because they are big. None of these designs can show that adding mentions *causes* AI visibility. What the data can do is rank levers — and raw links rank last among the things you could buy with the same budget.

**Seer's engine never searched.** The GPT‑4o API queries behind Seer's study ran without browsing, so every answer came from model memory. That makes it a clean measurement of the *parametric* case — which brands the model already knows — and a poor one for grounded answers. It also makes the weak backlink result almost mechanical: a link cannot influence an answer that retrieves nothing; only the text near it, absorbed into training data, can. Our own [42-day tracking study](/blog/citation-volatility) found ChatGPT grounded one definitional prompt on just 6 of 42 days, so the ungrounded case Seer measured is not an edge case — for many prompts it is the default, and mentions are the only lever that reaches it.

**Semrush's instrument partly measures the outcome.** Authority Score incorporates organic traffic. Correlating it with AI mentions therefore has a circularity problem the writeup does not dwell on: well-known sites score high and get mentioned, and no amount of link acquisition converts a small brand into a well-known site by moving the score alone.

**Three different denominators.** Ahrefs counted AI Overview presence for branded entities; Seer counted GPT‑4o mentions for finance and SaaS questions; Semrush blended five engines. As with the [rank-overlap estimates that range from 12% to 93%](/blog/rankings-and-ai-citations), the specific coefficients do not transfer across engines or categories — the *ordering* of factors is the durable finding, not the values.

## Should you cut your link-building budget?

No — you should change what it buys. Links still feed the rankings that retrieval-based engines draw on, and nothing in these datasets suggests crawlable, well-linked pages stopped mattering for classic search. What stopped surviving contact with the data is the classic KPI stack: referring-domain counts, followed-link premiums, exact-match anchors. The decision rule that falls out of the three studies:

1. **Pay for the description, not the attribute.** A placement's AI value lives in the sentence that names your brand and says what it is. Negotiate the wording; accept the nofollow. If a vendor's pricing tier is built on "dofollow guaranteed," you are buying a signal no answer engine reads.
2. **Put mentions where retrieval happens.** Pull the third-party domains that AI answers actually cite for your prompts — [across 25 million cited links, 84% pointed at domains the brand doesn't own](/blog/where-ai-citations-come-from) — and aim your PR at that list. A glowing mention on a page no engine retrieves competes only through training data, on a months-long lag.
3. **Split your prompts into grounded and ungrounded before allocating.** Where engines answer from memory, broad mention volume is the only lever; where they search, presence on the retrieved page is. Your own tracking data tells you which is which per prompt and per engine.
4. **Report the metric the engines respond to.** Branded web mentions and AI mention rate belong on the same slide as referring domains. If the link program grows the second column and not the first, it is running on 2015 objectives — the gap between the two is your [share of voice](/blog/ai-share-of-voice) problem hiding inside a healthy-looking link report.

The one-sentence version: build the same relationships, publish on the same sites, spend the same budget — but count mentions in retrievable text, because that is the asset the engines can actually see.
