**For two years, whether AI licensing deals affect AI search visibility has been a suspicion without a number. On 20 August 2026 it got one.** A joint study by Press Ranger and OtterlyAI, [announced via GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/08/20/3348287/0/en/press-ranger-and-otterlyai-release-study-showing-publishers-with-openai-deals-earn-48-more-ai-citations-on-chatgpt.html), matched 129.3 million AI citations from June 2026 against every confirmed licensing agreement between AI companies and news publishers. Pages from publishers with an OpenAI deal earned 48% more citations on ChatGPT than pages from publishers without one. Pages from publishers signed with OpenAI *exclusively* earned 112% more.

**Key takeaways**

- The premium is real and platform-specific: 10.2 citations per cited page on ChatGPT for OpenAI-licensed publishers versus 6.9 for unlicensed ones. Across all seven platforms measured, the gap narrows to 46% (10.7 versus 7.3).
- OpenAI is the only licensor whose deals pay on their own platform. Google's licensees were cited *slightly less* on AI Overviews than comparable unlicensed publishers; Perplexity's landed at parity on Perplexity.
- The deals reshape distribution, not just volume: OpenAI-licensed publishers now draw 57.9% of their entire AI citation volume from ChatGPT alone.
- For brands, the lever is indirect but usable — coverage in a licensed outlet sits on pages ChatGPT structurally favors, and the study's own advice is to earn coverage in vertical publishers engines already trust.
- Caveats are substantial: this is a vendor press release, not peer-reviewed research, the data covers one month, and the design is correlational. Read the direction, hold the decimals loosely.

## What was measured

The dataset is citation-side rather than survey-side: 129.3 million citations collected in June 2026 across ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, Google AI Mode, Perplexity, Microsoft Copilot, Gemini, and Claude, matched against the public record of licensing agreements between AI companies and news publishers.

The normalization matters. The study compares **citations per cited page**, not total citations — which strips out the most obvious objection, that licensed publishers are simply bigger and publish more. A page-level premium says that when a licensed publisher's page and an unlicensed publisher's page are both in the citable pool, ChatGPT returns to the licensed one more often.

| Comparison | Licensed | Unlicensed | Premium |
|---|---|---|---|
| ChatGPT, OpenAI-deal publishers | 10.2 citations/page | 6.9 citations/page | +48% |
| ChatGPT, OpenAI-exclusive publishers | — | — | +112% |
| All seven platforms, OpenAI cohort | 10.7 citations/page | 7.3 citations/page | +46% |

The exclusivity result is the sharpest edge in the data. Publishers signed with OpenAI and nobody else more than doubled the per-page citation rate of unlicensed publishers on ChatGPT — well above what the same publishers averaged elsewhere. Whatever mechanism connects a deal to citation behavior, it concentrates where the deal is.

## The asymmetry is the finding

If licensing deals simply made content more available — cleaner feeds, better crawl access — you'd expect every licensor's platform to favor its own licensees. That is not what the data shows.

| Licensor | Effect on its own platform |
|---|---|
| **OpenAI** | +48% citations per page on ChatGPT |
| **Google** | Slightly *below* comparable unlicensed publishers on AI Overviews |
| **Perplexity** | Parity on Perplexity |

Google's result is consistent with everything else known about AI Overviews: citation there tracks organic search systems, and [rank and AI citation already come apart](/blog/rankings-and-ai-citations) in measurable ways — a licensing agreement evidently doesn't reroute that pipeline. Perplexity's parity result is consistent with its revenue-sharing model, which pays publishers when cited rather than privileging them in retrieval.

ChatGPT is the outlier, and the study stops short of claiming intent. A deal typically bundles crawl permissions, structured content feeds, and sometimes prominence features, so the premium could be plumbing rather than favoritism. But the practical consequence is identical either way: the engine with the [largest share of AI search activity](/blog/ai-overviews-share-of-searches) has a measurable, deal-correlated skew in whose pages it cites.

The distribution effect compounds it. OpenAI-licensed publishers now take 57.9% of all their AI citations from ChatGPT alone, while unlicensed publishers keep a more balanced mix across ChatGPT and Perplexity. Thomas Peham, CEO of OtterlyAI, put it plainly: "A licensing deal does one clear thing: it tilts your citations toward ChatGPT."

## What this means if you are not a publisher

Almost nobody reading this can sign a content deal with OpenAI. The roughly 40 partnerships OpenAI has struck skew heavily toward large English-language premium news organizations, per [Press Gazette's analysis](https://pressgazette.co.uk/comment-analysis/publishers-with-ai-licensing-deals-have-seven-times-the-clickthrough-rate/) of Tollbit's State of the Bots data. The same analysis found licensed publishers enjoy roughly seven times the clickthrough rate from ChatGPT — the premium extends past citation into traffic.

But the premium attaches to *pages*, and your brand can appear on those pages. [Third-party sources already dominate where AI citations come from](/blog/where-ai-citations-come-from); this study adds a weighting to that map. A feature in an OpenAI-licensed outlet now sits on real estate ChatGPT structurally favors, while the same story in an unlicensed outlet of equal authority does not. For anyone allocating a PR budget with [answer engine optimization](/blog/answer-engine-optimization) in mind, that is a concrete, per-engine input into target selection — and a reason the licensed-outlet list is worth keeping next to your media list.

The equally useful half of the finding is negative: on AI Overviews and Perplexity, the licensed press has no edge. The study's own conclusion is that a deal "is not the only way to win" and that the smarter move for most organizations is earning coverage in the vertical publishers engines already trust. Trade press, category-specific outlets, and community sources remain the accessible tier — the [sourcing mix differs sharply per engine](/blog/where-ai-citations-come-from), and none of the non-ChatGPT engines pays a deal premium at all.

## What this does not show

The discounts here are larger than usual, and worth stating in order.

This is a press release from two companies with products to sell — Press Ranger distributes press releases and Otterly sells AI search monitoring — and "earned media drives AI citations" is a conclusion both benefit from. That doesn't make the numbers wrong, but no methodology paper accompanies them, nothing is peer-reviewed, and the matching of "comparable unlicensed publishers" is not documented.

The design is correlational with a built-in selection effect: OpenAI didn't license random publishers, it licensed prominent ones. Per-page normalization controls for volume, not for the underlying citability that made those publishers worth licensing in the first place. Some fraction of the 48% was there before any contract was signed.

The window is one month. Our own measurement of [citation volatility](/blog/citation-volatility) found the cited-domain set for a prompt turning over 60–70% day to day, so a June snapshot is a photograph of a fast-moving subject.

And a citation is not an endorsement of accuracy. The [Tow Center study covered by TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/11/29/study-of-chatgpt-citations-makes-dismal-reading-for-publishers/) found ChatGPT misattributing and inventing citations even for publishers with licensing deals. More citations per page says nothing about whether the answer described the page — or your brand — correctly.

## What to do on Monday

1. **Build the licensed-outlet list for your category.** The deals are public. Cross-reference them against the outlets that cover your vertical, and flag the overlap in your PR targeting.
2. **Split your earned-media targets per engine.** Licensed outlets for ChatGPT visibility; the organic-search-aligned playbook for AI Overviews; community and vertical sources for Perplexity. One media list serving three engines is now measurably leaving value on the table.
3. **Run the concentration check on your own citations.** If one engine supplies most of your AI visibility, you inherit its sourcing bias and its policy risk. The 57.9% figure is what over-concentration looks like from the inside.
4. **Watch for deal announcements in your category.** A competitor's favorite outlet signing with OpenAI changes the citation math for every brand that outlet covers, overnight, with no content change anywhere.
5. **Measure the answers, not just the citation counts.** The premium moves how often pages are cited; it does not police what the answers say about you. Accuracy remains your problem to monitor.

## Where this leaves the map

The clean version of the story — pay OpenAI, get cited — is not what the data shows. The messy version is more useful: one engine exhibits a large, deal-correlated citation skew; two engines exhibit none; and the deals themselves are reshaping where publishers' AI visibility concentrates. For brands, the actionable layer is that earned media now has engine-specific exchange rates, and the outlets that clear best on ChatGPT are, for the first time, a published list.
